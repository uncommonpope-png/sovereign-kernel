# Claim Calibration (State Claims Only As Strongly As The Evidence)

When you collapse many sources into one post, you smooth away the jaggedness of the evidence and assert a cleaner conclusion than the citations support. A cited claim can still be wrong — wrong in strength. Never let a post sound more certain than the evidence you actually read.

## Use When
- Before posting any claim that rests on sources, studies, or reports
- When a draft states a conclusion, a trend, or a general rule
- When a draft uses absolute words: "proves", "always", "never", "definitively", "science says", "everyone knows"
- After verify-before-posting passes a claim as true — this checks how strongly it is stated, not whether it is true

## How It Works
1. List every factual claim in the draft. For each, note the exact source passage you actually opened and read — not a source you cited from memory or from another summary.
2. Match strength to evidence. One study is "one study found", never "science proves". One setting is "in this setting", never "always". A correlation is "linked to", never "causes".
3. Hunt the three smoothing patterns and rewrite each one you find:
   - Unsupported composition: separately supported pieces stitched into a stronger conclusion than any single source supports. Split it back into its pieces, or soften the conclusion to what the pieces jointly support.
   - Scope expansion: a result from one setting, group, or time stated as general. Put the scope back in the sentence.
   - Premature closure: "definitively", "fundamentally resolves", "settles it" — language that closes a question the evidence leaves open. Replace with what is actually known and what is still open.
4. Open every cited page. If you have not opened a cited source, either open it and re-check the claim, or drop the citation and soften the claim. A citation to a page you never read is decoration, not evidence.
5. If the evidence is thin, say so in the post or drop the claim. "I don't know" and "the evidence is mixed" are successful outcomes, not failures.

## Prerequisites
- The draft post and the sources it rests on
- verify-before-posting already run (truth first, strength second)

## Notes
- Evidence: "Quantifying Claim Smoothing by LLM Research Agents" (arXiv:2610.35793, 26 Oct 2026) — across 7 research-agent systems, at least 22% of claims were materially misleading on every system; one agent opened only 19% of the documents it cited, another 2%; in many reports no cited page was opened at all. Giving the agent the full cited documents AND calibration instructions cut materially smoothed claims from 29% to 12% and 61% to 25% — either alone did not help.
